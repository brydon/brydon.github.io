# Switchback cabin proof of concept

One small, fully 3D scene, with an authored entrance and a real website on the computer inside. The mountain valley sets the scene; the cabin is the experience.

The exterior has a timber cabin, Canadian flag, fire ring, front steps, a black 2015 Iron 883 and Brydon in a grey plaid flannel. All are modeled geometry. The helmet rests on the porch. Entering opens the hinged door, walks Brydon to it and takes the camera inside. WASD, arrow keys and on-screen controls pan the camera within comfortable limits.

The interior follows the approved “After Hours” reference: a left-wall bookshelf, unobstructed math chalkboard, climbing ropes and helmet, coffee setup, open stone fireplace, woven rug, sleeping Bernese and a working desk. Plants, a thrown blanket, firewood, pencils and lighting make the room feel inhabited.

Clicking anywhere on the monitor approaches its screen. A same-origin HTML iframe, positioned with Three.js CSS3DRenderer, sits behind a transparent WebGL opening. Once the camera settles, the screen's transforms flatten to a matching 2D rectangle for reliable browser hit testing. The same iframe remains mounted; moving between views does not reload it. Native links, text selection, scrolling, page navigation and terminal input work on the actual screen. Input is enabled only after the approach finishes. Escape or the Room button returns to the cabin.

Henry Heffernan's desk and monitor interaction and Bruno Simon's memorable landing scene informed the direction. Geometry and interaction code here are originally authored; Three.js is the rendering dependency, including its CSS3DRenderer addon. No reference-site source or assets were copied.

The original site supplies personal details and the research archive. Direct reading links preserve access without playing through the scene or needing a working GPU. Sound is opt-in; reduced motion skips travel and stops environmental animations.

The Base64 note is carried forward. A harder SHA-256 puzzle validates a phrase assembled from the coffee mug, motorcycle and outdoor fire. It is optional. The terminal implements a finite list of commands, with no eval, filesystem access or network side effects.

## Discoveries

The SHA-256 unlock now changes the persistent world state: aurora ribbons, stars, fireflies and strings of warm cabin lights appear. The camera returns outside to reveal the midnight valley. The service label is a separate raster PNG, with no encoded phrase in its HTML or JavaScript; the decoded answer is checked against a digest and rewards the actual administrator of the cabin.

Several finite terminal commands are deliberately absent from help. The destructive-looking command is purely theatrical: the monitor bursts, the dog evacuates, the cabin catches fire, the roof collapses and the walls char. After Brydon has left the cabin and walked onto the path, the scene waits three seconds before opening a replacement-shopping search in a new tab. A matching link stays available in browsers that block automatic new tabs. The original scene keeps burning; Rebuild cabin reloads it. The terminal never runs shell commands or removes files.

The welcome and navigation fade after the first world interaction, leaving the name. Objects use invisible accessible click areas; there are no floating instructional labels. Outside discoveries wait until Brydon has walked to them. The bike is parked along the right wall facing out, log seats surround the fire, and a Muskoka chair with coffee sits on the porch. A pen cup holds Canadian and Sri Lankan flags, with a hidden note for Brydon’s wife. Clicking the dog lifts its head, wags its tail and raises a small 3D heart. Lighting uses the visitor’s local hour unless manually overridden.

The right wall now has an actual window opening onto the scene’s moon and trees, beside a pegboard with a bundled rope, quickdraws, harness, chalk bag, belay device and brush. The chalkboard is repositioned clear of the left bookcase.
